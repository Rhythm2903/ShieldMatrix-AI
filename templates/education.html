1. Phishing & Suspicious Links

The Threat

Phishing is a type of social engineering where attackers deceive you into revealing sensitive information or installing malware by disguising themselves as a trustworthy entity. Malicious links often hide behind fake "Free Giftcard" offers or urgent "Account Compromised" alerts.

How to Prevent It

  • Hover before you click: Always hover your mouse over a link to inspect the actual URL before clicking.
  • Verify the source: If an email or message claims urgency, navigate to the official website manually rather than clicking the provided link.
  • Look for typos: Phishing sites often use misspelled domains (e.g., goog1e.com instead of google.com).

2. Malicious Downloads (Malware & Ransomware)

The Threat

Downloading unverified files (like random `.vbs`, `.exe`, or macro-enabled `.doc` files) can execute malicious code on your computer. This malicious data can spy on your keystrokes, steal passwords, or lock your files until a ransom is paid.

How to Prevent It

  • Only trust official sources: Never download software from third-party distribution sites. Go directly to the developer's official page.
  • Check file extensions: Be highly suspicious of unexpected script files (like `.vbs` or `.bat`) masquerading as invoices or documents.
  • Keep defenses active: Ensure your OS and antivirus definitions are up to date to catch known malicious signatures.

3. Unsecured Connections & Data Interception

The Threat

Submitting sensitive data (like usernames and passwords) over an unencrypted HTTP connection means your data is transmitted in plain text. Any attacker monitoring the network (like on public Wi-Fi) can easily intercept and steal this information.

How to Prevent It

  • Look for HTTPS: Ensure the website URL begins with https:// and has a padlock icon in the browser address bar before entering any credentials.
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive tasks: Do not check bank accounts or log into critical services on unsecured public networks without a trusted VPN.
  • Use a Password Manager: This prevents you from accidentally typing credentials into HTTP or cloned sites, as the manager won't recognize the fake domain.
